# Veltex Qualified Growth and Product Expansion Plan

Date: 2026-09-20  
Status: active implementation plan

## Product standard

Veltex does not claim to identify a universally correct bid. It helps a
cleaning operator understand, document, revise, and defend a profitable bid.
Every suggested price must expose its assumptions and remain subject to the
operator's review.

## Priority 1 — Qualified-owner Meta challenger

Keep V13 unchanged as the control. Build a separate challenger whose creative
explicitly addresses cleaning-company owners, managers, and estimators. The
challenger must collect role, business type, and bidding-frequency signals and
optimize toward a deeper first-party milestone once volume permits. Landing-page
views alone are not the success metric.

The challenger may be prepared without incremental spend. Publishing requires
an explicit daily budget/allocation because it either adds spend or changes the
control's budget.

Primary metric: cost per qualified cleaning-business decision-maker who starts
a trial and saves a first proposal.

## Priority 2 — Production funnel proof

The production release records successful generation, first/repeat saved
proposals, and checkout starts. The next genuine signup should be used to verify
registration and trial delivery in Meta and the first-party funnel. Synthetic
QA identities remain excluded from growth reporting.

## Priority 3 — Suggested bid guidance

Customer-facing results use “suggested range,” “working bid guidance,” and
“planning assumptions.” The operator reviews scope, wages, labor burden,
production, supplies, equipment, travel, overhead, contingency, and target
margin before presenting a price.

Future calculation output should include low/base/high scenarios, labor hours,
burdened labor, non-labor costs, overhead, contingency, target margin, monthly
and annual value, and warnings when an override falls below modeled cost.

## Priority 4 — Shared cleaning-service architecture

Use three composable layers rather than separate one-off builders:

1. Business segment — who performs the work.
2. Property/job profile — where and under what conditions it is performed.
3. Service modules — measurable tasks, frequencies, resources, and risks.

Rollout order is commercial janitorial; residential/vacation rental; interior
specialty; exterior services; then regulated specialty work. Regulated services
require separate safety, qualification, and jurisdiction review before release.

## Priority 5 — Walkthrough media

Create a private job-media intake flow for photos and short videos. AI may draft
observations, questions, scope candidates, and add-on suggestions. Every result
must be labeled as an AI observation and confirmed by the operator. Media alone
must not silently determine dimensions, contamination, compliance, or price.

Required safeguards: customer consent, tenant isolation, signed access,
retention/deletion controls, metadata stripping, face/document redaction,
malware/type/size validation, and an audit trail from media observation to an
operator-approved scope item.

## Acceptance gates

- No unsupported “correct,” “perfect,” or guaranteed-price claim.
- No new service appears in the UI without real inputs and calculation rules.
- No regulated module launches on generic janitorial assumptions.
- No AI media observation enters a proposal without confirmation.
- No campaign is judged by cheap traffic when qualified-owner outcomes exist.
